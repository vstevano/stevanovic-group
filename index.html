Computational Materials Physics

Discovering and designing functional inorganic materials

We use first-principles theory to search for new functional materials and to understand why they form the structures they do and why they have the properties they do.

Research Areas

Stability and metastability

First-principles coumpound thermochemistry and synthesizability — accurate formation energies, random structure sampling of polymorphs, polymorphic transformations and transformation kinetics, and prredicting which metastable structures are realizable in the lab.

More →

Amorphous materials

Amorphous state as an ensemble of small-cell local domains, an approach enabling high-accuracy GW/BSE calculations of electronic structure, transport, and optical properties of disordered systems.

More →

Wide gap semiconductors

Wide- and ultrawide-bandgap oxide and nitride semiconductors for high-power electronics — candidate discovery, dopability, and interfaces.

More →

Other stuff

Defects and doping, photovoltaic and thermoelectric materials, and the computational methods and software that tie it all together.

About the group

Stevanović group started in 2013 at Colorado School of Mines and the National Renewable Energy Laboratory, now National Laboratory of the Rockies (NLR) in Golden Colorado. Group's research stands at the intersections of solid-state physiscs, materials science, and large-scale computations. PI Vladan Stevanović is a theoretical solid-state physicist and Professor at Colorado School of Mines, with a joint appointment at NLR. The group has advised 13 PhD students, 4 undergraduate researchers, and 8 postdoctoral researchers, and has published more than 120 papers in leading physics, chemistry and materials science journals.
